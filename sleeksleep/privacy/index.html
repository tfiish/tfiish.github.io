SleekSleep Privacy Policy

Last updated: 2026-11-14

SleekSleep collects no data. It has no accounts, no server, no analytics, no ads, and no third-party code, and it never sends anything off your iPhone on its own. Nothing is sold, shared, or used for advertising.

What SleekSleep reads and writes

SleekSleep works only with Apple Health, and only with the sleep data you allow it to use.

  • It writes the sleep times you log, as Sleep entries in Apple Health. Each entry also notes that you entered it by hand, how you logged it (with the widget, or in the app by wake time or by duration), the time zone your iPhone was in when it was first saved, and which version of SleekSleep last saved it, along with an ID and an edit count that let SleekSleep update it in place. Like any sleep entry, these can be seen by other apps you allow to read your Sleep data.
  • It reads your Sleep data from Apple Health—including sleep saved by other apps and devices, if you allow it—to show your history and charts, last night’s sleep on the widget, the times it suggests when you log, whether a new or edited entry overlaps sleep already there, and whether today’s reminder is still needed.

All charts are worked out on your device while the app is open. SleekSleep keeps no copy of your sleep history. The little it holds on your iPhone, and for how long, is below.

What stays on your iPhone

SleekSleep keeps the following on your iPhone, in storage that only SleekSleep can read:

  • Your settings, such as your reminder time and sleep target.
  • An in-progress entry that hasn’t reached Apple Health yet (like an in-progress sleep, or an entry the SleekSleep widget couldn’t save). Each is removed once it’s saved to Apple Health or discarded.
  • A night the widget has just logged, kept for the few minutes the widget offers Undo, then removed the next time the widget refreshes or you open SleekSleep.

What iOS keeps

iOS keeps some things for every app, outside SleekSleep’s control:

  • If you back up your iPhone, in iCloud or on a computer, the backup includes SleekSleep’s settings, anything still waiting to be saved, and a night the widget has just logged.
  • To show them quickly, iOS keeps a picture of the app’s last screen, and may keep one of the SleekSleep widget.
  • While you’re sharing an export, iOS may also hold its own copy until the share is done.

What happens to these is set by iOS and your backup settings, not by SleekSleep.

Exporting your sleep

If you choose Export CSV beside Download sleep data in Settings, SleekSleep makes a CSV file of the sleep it can read from Apple Health and hands it to the iOS share sheet. The file is kept in SleekSleep’s temporary storage while the share sheet is open, and SleekSleep deletes it when the sheet closes. If that’s missed, such as when the app quits first, it’s deleted the next time you open SleekSleep. Where the file goes (such as AirDrop, an email, or your Files app) is your choice.

Reminders

Reminders are scheduled by iOS on your iPhone. Like any app’s notifications, iOS may also show them on your other devices, such as a paired Apple Watch or a Mac using iPhone Mirroring. SleekSleep itself sends nothing anywhere.

Crash reports and usage figures

If you’ve chosen in iOS Settings to share analytics with app developers, Apple may pass us crash reports and usage figures, such as how often the app is opened. They describe what the app was doing, not your sleep.

Apple Health and iCloud

SleekSleep doesn’t use iCloud itself. If you’ve turned on Health in your iCloud settings, Apple syncs your Health data, including the entries SleekSleep saved, under the Apple Health privacy policy rather than this one.

Your choices

  • Edit or delete an entry SleekSleep logged in the app’s History, or delete it in the Health app.
  • Change or turn off SleekSleep’s access in Settings → Privacy & Security → Health → SleekSleep.
  • Delete the app to remove its settings and anything still waiting to be saved. Entries already saved stay in Apple Health until you delete them there.
